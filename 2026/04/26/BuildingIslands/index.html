DevLog: Building Islands and Seabeds from Scratch – Prototyping Environments with Free Prefabs

If the past few weeks of wrestling with UDP network broadcasts and UI state machines were about laying the foundation, the work over these past few days has been about actually “building the house” on top of it.

With the network multiplayer and the pause menu (featuring the network diagnostic panel) running smoothly, our project has finally crossed the deep waters of underlying technology and officially entered the level blockout/prototyping phase. Today’s log is about the birth of the basic foundation of our game world—the islands and the seabed.

🏝️ Charting the Course: Starting and Destination Islands

Our core gameplay loop unfolds across different islands. To quickly validate the gameplay, my primary goal for this phase was to construct two key landmarks: the Starting Island and the Destination Island.

As a student team, modeling everything from scratch is obviously unrealistic and would severely slow down our progress. Therefore, I adopted a common rapid prototyping strategy used in the industry: clever use of free prefabs.

By hunting down high-quality free environment assets (rocks, vegetation, terrain materials, etc.) from the Unity Asset Store and open-source communities, I was able to snap together the outlines of two distinctly styled islands like Lego blocks.

  • Starting Island: Primarily serves as the spawn point, tutorial area, and initial resource gathering zone. The terrain is relatively flat, visually providing the player with a sense of safety.
  • Destination Island: Acting as the milestone for the voyage, its terrain design is more complex. Viewed from the open ocean, it needs strong visual guidance (such as taller peaks or a unique silhouette).

🌊 Bridging the Gap: Sculpting the Seabed

The two islands don’t exist in isolation; they are connected by a vast ocean. To ensure the voyage isn’t monotonous, I also built the Seabed topography connecting the two landmasses.

Using Unity’s Terrain tools combined with prefabs of underwater rocks and coral, I sculpted an undulating underwater landscape.

Here, a previous technical achievement really shined:
The URP dynamic underwater visual effects (global fog and depth testing) that I spent so much effort on earlier had their highlight moment here! When the scene was assembled and the camera dove underwater, the rough edges of the model seams were perfectly softened by the underwater fog. Sunlight refracted through the water surface onto the uneven seabed, instantly maximizing the scene’s immersion. At that moment, I truly felt like the world had come alive.

🚀 Phase Summary and Next Steps

Using free prefabs for white-boxing/prototyping has immensely optimized my workflow. It allowed me to focus my precious energy on the “Level Flow” and a sense of spatial scale, rather than getting bogged down tweaking the normal map of a single rock.

Currently, the basic scene structure of the game is established. The two islands and the seabed not only provide physical space for the upcoming sailing gameplay but also serve as an excellent testing playground for our cross-platform LAN multiplayer.

In the next Sprint, we will drop the finalized Player Controller into this scene to conduct real movement and multiplayer interaction tests. The stage for our voyage is set—it’s time to set sail!
